Eye Strain: Causes, Symptoms, and Simple Exercises to Relieve It

If you’ve ever spent hours reading, working on a computer, or scrolling through your phone and ended up with burning eyes, blurry vision, or a headache, you’ve probably experienced eye strain.

Eye strain is extremely common, especially with the amount of time many of us spend looking at screens every day. The good news is that it is usually temporary and can often be improved by giving your eyes regular breaks and changing your visual focus.

In this article, we’ll look at what causes eye strain, its most common symptoms, and some simple exercises you can use to give your eyes a break.

What Is Eye Strain?

Eye strain, sometimes called digital eye strain when it is related to prolonged screen use, happens when your eyes become tired from extended visual activity.

Your eyes aren’t designed to stare at the same distance for hours without a break. When you’re reading, working on a computer, or looking at your phone, your eyes continuously work to keep things in focus. At the same time, you tend to blink less and maintain your attention on one area for long periods.

This combination can eventually leave your eyes feeling tired, dry, irritated, or uncomfortable.

Eye strain isn’t necessarily caused by screens themselves. Any activity that requires prolonged visual concentration can contribute to it, including reading a book, driving long distances, studying, sewing, or working with small objects.

What Causes Eye Strain?

Several factors can contribute to eye strain.

1. Prolonged screen use

Computers, phones, and tablets are among the most common causes of modern eye strain. When using a screen, people tend to stare continuously and blink less frequently.

This can cause the tear film covering your eyes to evaporate more quickly, leading to dryness and irritation.

2. Focusing at one distance for too long

When you look at something close, your eyes use their focusing system to maintain a clear image. Reading or looking at a phone for a long time means that this system remains engaged for an extended period.

Looking into the distance allows the focusing system to relax, which is one reason why taking regular distance breaks can feel refreshing.

3. Reduced blinking

We naturally blink less when concentrating on something, particularly when using digital devices.

Blinking spreads tears across the surface of your eyes and helps keep them lubricated. When you blink less, your eyes can become dry and irritated.

4. Poor lighting and glare

Working in very dim lighting, dealing with screen glare, or having excessive contrast between your screen and surroundings can make visual tasks more demanding.

5. Uncorrected vision problems

If you need glasses or your prescription is no longer appropriate, your eyes may have to work harder to maintain clear vision. This can contribute to eye fatigue, headaches, and difficulty concentrating.

6. Poor posture and viewing distance

How you position yourself can also contribute to discomfort. Sitting too close to a screen or spending long periods with your head and neck in an awkward position can contribute to headaches, neck pain, shoulder pain, and back discomfort alongside eye strain.

What Are the Symptoms of Eye Strain?

Eye strain doesn’t only affect your eyes. It can also cause symptoms elsewhere in your body.

Symptoms affecting your eyes

Common eye-related symptoms include:

  • Watery eyes
  • Dry eyes
  • Blurred or fluctuating vision
  • Sensitivity to light
  • Burning or irritated eyes
  • Itchy eyes
  • Difficulty keeping your eyes open
  • A feeling of tired or heavy eyes

You may notice that some of these symptoms appear after a long period of reading or screen use and improve after resting your eyes.

Symptoms affecting the rest of your body

Eye strain can also be associated with:

  • Headaches
  • Neck pain
  • Shoulder pain
  • Back pain
  • Difficulty concentrating
  • General fatigue

This is one reason prolonged screen sessions can leave you feeling tired even when you haven’t been physically active.

Best Exercises and Habits for Eye Strain

You don’t need complicated exercises to give your eyes a break. Some of the simplest techniques can be done almost anywhere.

1. Blinking and yawning

It sounds almost too simple, but blinking is one of the easiest ways to help your eyes stay comfortable.

When you’re concentrating, consciously remind yourself to blink fully and regularly. Blinking helps spread the tear film across your eye and can reduce dryness.

Yawning naturally causes your eyes to squeeze and water, which can also help refresh the surface of your eyes.

Try taking a few slow, complete blinks whenever you notice that you’ve been staring at your screen for a while.

2. Focus changing

One of the most useful habits during prolonged near work is changing where you look.

After spending time looking at your phone or computer, look at something farther away. This allows your eyes’ focusing system to relax rather than remaining focused at the same close distance.

You can try this simple exercise:

  1. Look at something close for several seconds.
  2. Move your gaze to an object farther away.
  3. Focus on the distant object for around 20 seconds.
  4. Repeat when needed.

A commonly recommended approach is the 20-20-20 rule: every 20 minutes, look at something approximately 20 feet (6 meters) away for at least 20 seconds.

3. Palming

Close your eyes and gently place your warm palms over them without pressing on your eyeballs.

Stay like this for around 20–30 seconds while relaxing your face and breathing normally.

The goal isn’t to “strengthen” your eyes, but simply to give them a short period without visual stimulation.

4. Near-to-far focusing

You can deliberately alternate between a nearby object and something in the distance.

For example, look at your finger or another object a short distance away for a few seconds, then shift your attention to something across the room or outside a window.

Repeat this several times.

This is essentially a more deliberate version of taking distance breaks during normal screen use.

5. Take regular breaks

Perhaps the most important “exercise” is simply stopping the visual task for a moment.

Stand up, look around the room, look out a window, stretch your body, and let your eyes move naturally rather than keeping them fixed on a screen.

Short, frequent breaks are generally more useful than waiting until your eyes already feel exhausted.

Give Your Eyes a Break

Eye strain is often a result of doing something simple for too long: looking at the same thing without giving your eyes enough opportunities to rest and refocus.

If you spend a large part of your day on screens, small habits can make a difference. Blink regularly, adjust your lighting, maintain a comfortable viewing distance, change your focus, and take frequent breaks.

You don’t need to wait until your eyes start burning to take a break.

Sometimes, the best thing you can do for your eyes is simply look away.
